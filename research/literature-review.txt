Literature and priority check: four-colour sources, three-colour targets
Checked 1 November 2026

Conclusion
The sharp threshold 29 and the two labelled extremal 28-cube multisets
were not found in the sources examined. The precise threshold is explicitly
asked for in Problem 2 of Berkove, Eickemeyer and Schulman (2018).
This is a bounded literature search, not a certification of priority.

Primary sources examined

1. E. Berkove, A. Eickemeyer and M. Schulman, The 4-Color Cubes Puzzle,
   Integers 18 (2018), A68.
   https://math.colgate.edu/~integers/s68/s68.pdf
   Section 8, Problem 2 asks when four-colour source cubes can build a
   three-colour corner solution and asks for the minimum guaranteed size.
   Its earlier threshold eleven concerns four-colour targets. Its minimum
   universal corner-collection size ten is already proved, not an open
   problem. Section 2 and Table 8 supply the corner-containment reduction.

2. E. Berkove and J. Schettler, The corner poset with an application to
   an n-dimensional hypercube stacking puzzle, Australasian Journal of
   Combinatorics 84(1) (2022), 86–110.
   https://ajc.maths.uq.edu.au/pdf/84/ajc_v84_p086.pdf
   This studies two-colour hypercubes in varying dimension. Its overview
   distinguishes earlier results by source palette size; it does not
   provide the four-to-three-colour threshold studied here.

3. I. Johnson and E. Roldán, Solution Numbers for Eight Blocks to Madness
   Puzzle, arXiv:2407.13208 (2024).
   https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.13208
   https://doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.2407.13208
   The scope is six distinct face colours, principally collections of
   distinct cubes and target counts/universal collections. It does not
   answer the four-to-three-colour question. The arXiv record examined
   lists the original 18 July 2024 version.

4. R. A. Beeler, Major MacMahon's Revenge, author-hosted survey.
   https://faculty.etsu.edu/beelerr/macmahon-revenge.pdf
   Full PDF retrieved and converted to text after browser retrieval failed.
   Its cube discussion describes the classical six-colour puzzle; most of
   the article concerns edge-matching polygon puzzles. It cites the 2018
   four-colour paper but does not give the present threshold/classification.

Discovery checks
The Lafayette REU publications list confirms the 2017 two/three-colour
chapter and 2018 four-colour paper. The 2017 six-colour paper's references,
the 2022 paper's references, institutional publication pages, and indexed
title/citation searches were used to look for follow-up work. Queries
included combinations of the exact 2018 title, the authors' names,
four-/three-colour corner solutions, and twenty-nine/29-cube thresholds.
These searches returned no matching later result. Search engine coverage
and institutional lists are incomplete; snippets were not treated as proof
that an unread article cannot contain the result.

different palette setting, but is only indirect evidence about the unread
