Date: 2026-11-01

Conclusion
I found no mathematical error or unresolved gap in the threshold-29 proof
or the classification of the two labelled 28-cube obstructions. The
argument appears valid under its stated model: a fixed four-colour palette,
source cubes using every colour, proper rotations only, repeated varieties
allowed, and unrestricted internal faces. The proof, rather than the
reported optimisation or samples, establishes completeness.

Reasoning audited
* Corner containment has the correct direction: replacing a source by one
  with fewer available corner types preserves the absence of a solution.
  The seven colour-permutation orbits cover all 68 varieties, and each
  discarded orbit admits the printed checkerboard replacement. Consequently
  absence of checkerboards after reduction excludes discarded varieties
  from the original extremal collection.
* The bucket lemma's incidence estimate and its two-demand Hall conditions
  suffice; each selected physical cube occupies only one position.
* The checkerboard argument is sound. First z_E <= 7 gives A >= 7;
  B+z_E >= 4 would already solve an opposite-pair target, so C+2B <= 6
  and A >= 22. Bucket counting gives P <= C. A checkerboard then forces
  every t_a <= 5, hence 4 <= C <= 6. The asserted checkerboard supporting
  a triple through any prescribed pair exists: otherwise all checkerboards
  have that pair as their singleton colours, forcing some z_E=C >= 4.
  Both subsequent R constructions use distinct cubes, with an unused
  larger-class cube available. Their strengthened bucket inequalities give
  T+P <= 20, contradicting T+P >= 22.
* With C=P=0, all four t_a equal seven. If both orientation classes occur,
  choosing a repeated colour different from that of the selected minority
  cube keeps the six supporting T cubes distinct from it. This constructs
  R and proves the one-class conclusion. The deletion/insertion argument
  then rules out 29-cube obstructions.
* The dual-octahedron boundary argument correctly balances trichromatic
  corner orientations. The accompanying classification of targets without
  such corners covers both singleton-colour and 2+2+2 face distributions.
  It proves that the displayed 28-cube examples fail for every target.

Minor exposition/checker clarifications, not proof defects
The note could explicitly say that each ordinary orientation class contains
one T variety per repeated colour; this is implicit in its geometric
description and was independently checked above. The checker's output
field "labelled_lower_obstructions": 2 counts the two exhibited inventories
in a colour-permutation orbit. Its finite checks alone do not classify all
28-cube inventories; that completeness comes from the written proof.

Follow-up: Lean formalisation scope and trust audit (2026-11-01)

Conclusion: I found no weakened hypothesis, omitted cube-use constraint,
or incorrect physical compatibility condition in the formal definitions
or the inspected counting/classification proofs. This remains an AI review.

Definitions and theorem scope
* Inventory is Fin 68 -> Nat, with no bound on the multiplicity of an
  individual variety. Stock is the dependent pair (i, j) with j < x i.
  Builds requires an embedding from all eight slots into Stock. Distinct
  slots therefore use distinct copies, including when varieties repeat.
* Hall quantifies over every finite subset of the eight slots and counts
  the full stock multiplicity of its source neighbourhood. The proved
  hall_iff_builds applies Mathlib's finite Hall theorem to these actual
  copies. It does not treat a cube's multiple corners as multiple cubes.
* PhysicalSolvable selects one target for the entire assembly, an injective
  placement of eight copies, and one rotation per occupied position. The
  same rotation matches all three outward faces of that cube. There is no
  constraint on the internal faces, consistently with the stated puzzle.
  MacMahon's rotation characterization uses distinct coordinate axes and
  determinant +1, and exposed_face_rule describes all eight binary slots.
* fits_physical checks all 68 sources, 120 targets, and eight positions;
  solvable_iff_physical transports the full matching problem in both
  directions. Exact-three-colour target and exact-four-colour source
  conditions are proved for every catalogue entry in Encoder.lean.
* The counting proof operates on the original 68 source indices, not just
  the 38 reduced types. Its partition has eight T indices, 24 P indices,
  and 36 checker-group indices; the last group includes all 30 discarded
  varieties. All statistical partition identities and construction
  certificates are checked against these original groups and actual fits.
  Thus no unproved assumption that an arbitrary inventory is reduced is
  hidden in threshold29 or classify28.
* Certificate universally quantifies over every Hall subset and supplies a
  pool contained in its neighbourhood with sufficient cardinality. The
  construction lemmas prove the required inventory mass bounds. They do
  not trust a solver's assertion of feasibility or an unchecked matching.
  The lower witnesses likewise have a checked Hall deficiency for every
  one of the 120 targets.

Precise representation scope
The final wrappers quantify over multiplicity functions on the 68 fixed
catalogue representatives. Catalogue.lean proves coverage of every raw
four-colour source and every exactly-three-colour target up to a proper
rotation, using all 4^6 face assignments. Uniqueness.lean now additionally
proves that distinct source indices are distinct proper-rotation varieties.
The main theorem statements use this canonical-variety inventory model;
they are not statements directly over lists of arbitrarily oriented raw
face assignments. The formalisation guide accurately states this scope.
I independently verified the 68 source and 120 target rotation orbits.
There is no remaining source-uniqueness qualification. The final
least_guaranteed_size theorem also establishes the full minimality claim:
29 is the least n for which every inventory of total size at least n is
physically solvable, rather than only asserting sufficiency at exactly 29.
