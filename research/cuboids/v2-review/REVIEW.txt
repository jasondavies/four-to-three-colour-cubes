ASSESSMENT

No mathematical defect was found in the written cube, corner, stability,
counting, thin-cuboid or all-cuboid results reviewed here. The main v2
improvement is presentational: make the general cuboid theorem the organising
result, and keep the stronger cube classification as a distinct contribution.
The proof's finite geometry, physical interpretation, arithmetic endpoints and
small-width cases were checked separately. This is an AI-assisted review and
fresh computational audit, not external human refereeing or a new priority
search.

The local revision changes documentation, the presentation of one proof,
two CI settings, and an audit-scope sentence. No Lean theorem or proof source
was changed. The revised manuscript compiles to 16 pages, down from 17. Its
final LaTeX pass has no warnings, unresolved references or box problems; all
16 pages were rendered and visually checked. The README is approximately
39% shorter (about 2,100 to 1,300 whitespace-delimited words).

PRINCIPAL EDITORIAL CHANGES

1. The title is now 'Three-colour cuboids from four-colour cubes', including
   PDF metadata and formalization.yaml. Filenames remain stable.

2. The README starts with the v2 addition, links to the actual archived v1,
   and defines sources, targets, F and G before presenting the formulas.
   The source model specifies all four fixed-palette colours, repeated
   varieties, proper rotations, monochromatic target faces in exactly three
   colours, and unrestricted interior faces.

3. A six-row table distinguishes frame size, volume, frame threshold and
   complete-cuboid threshold. The examples with 28 frame positions but
   thresholds 33 and 29 make the role of shape concrete.

4. The README's proof overview now describes the cuboid construction: the
   weighted-triangle D branch, then reservations and Hall matching in the
   no-D branch. Its former overview described only cube stability.

5. 'Ordinary cubes' is no longer used to mean equal-sided targets. 'Ordinary'
   retains its technical meaning for the T/P source varieties; targets with
   equal sides are called cubic targets or cubic frames.

6. The all-cuboid no-D proof has one general case and one exceptional case,
   replacing the previous a>=3, b=5, b<=4 and two-high-colour subdivisions.
   The mathematical ingredients and theorem remain the same.

7. Repeated discussion of verification scope was removed from the paper's
   main text, while the verification appendix retains the comparator and
   physical-model details. The abstract states the physical target condition
   explicitly. The roadmap explains that the cuboid proof needs the shared
   geometry, not the earlier threshold or classification theorems.

8. Detailed audit and CP-SAT instructions were moved into ThreeColour/README.md.
   The root README retains the primary build commands, theorem map and the
   explicit distinction between library proofs and comparator replay.
   Time-limited solver runs may return UNKNOWN; they are no longer described
   as unconditionally reporting INFEASIBLE.

9. formalization.yaml now says 'all-cuboid thresholds', replacing 'full cuboid
   classification'. General cuboid extremal inventories are not classified;
   the paper correctly leaves them as a further question.

10. Theorem 2 is kept with the beginning of its proof rather than being left
    alone at the bottom of a page. The figures, tables and all mathematical
    labels remain intact.

MATHEMATICAL REVIEW

For ordered integer dimensions 2 <= a <= b <= c, the reviewed formulas are

  F = max(16a-3, 4(a+b+c)-16, floor((24c-1)/5)),
  G = max(abc, 16a-3, floor((24c-1)/5)).

The three lower bounds are sound. A one-orientation-class T inventory forces
Q and falls one short of the 4a required copies in a bucket. The position
count gives the cardinality bound. Balanced checkerboards force D; the eight
corners and the four longest edges jointly require 4c sources containing one
bichromatic pair. Counting the corners in that demand is essential, and the
argument does so correctly for every axis assignment.

The weighted-triangle lemma was checked under its actual weaker hypothesis
N >= max(29,L,K(c)), without silently adding the shortest-side bound. The
light-edge and heavy-edge cases work. Assigning the selected triangle's
colour pairs to axes is legitimate because the target itself is chosen by
the construction; source cubes still use proper rotations.

The residual Hall argument is sufficient: each individual type has enough
suppliers, and the union of any two distinct bichromatic-type supplier sets
contains the entire residual inventory. Together with the total position
bound, these are all the grouped Hall inequalities required.

The Q and H directional demands and their reservations were verified from
actual integer-coordinate cuboids. H reserves 2a majority T_u copies, b
majority T_v copies, one supporting minority copy, and one further majority
copy. The extra-copy budget and the inequality N >= 4a+2b+2c-5 are sufficient.

In the shortened no-D argument, n_T >= 16a-14 guarantees some t_u >= 2a.
If at most one colour has fewer than b T copies, a supported minority triple
through u provides a second suitable colour. Otherwise n_T <= 11a forces
a=2. Then

  16b-56 <= 2L-40 <= 2N-40 <= n_T <= 2b+12

forces b<=4. The bounds n_T<=20 and n_T>=30-2M force M>=5. Two colours have
at least four T copies each, and a minority source supports their pair,
supplying the same H construction. This retains the original proof's
physical reservations while shortening the case split.

Completion is valid because every position outside the frame exposes at
most one face when all sides are at least two. The exact-cardinality
inequalities and the four non-rod volume exceptions have the correct integer
endpoints. The side-one case remains outside the theorem.

The cube review checked orientation balance, the 28-cube classification,
the 29-cube swap argument, D-frame extension, the U reservation, the stability
criterion, the weak-composition bijection, inclusion-maximality, and the
n=2 endpoint of the 32-case near-extremal classification. In particular,
t_a >= h-d > 0 prevents hidden feasibility restrictions in the counting
parametrisation. No correction to these results was needed.

The original BES paper was checked: its Problem 2, Theorem 5 and bucket
attribution support the manuscript's claims. The archived v1 already contains
the cube stability and counting results, so the all-cuboid thresholds are the
precise new v2 contribution.

No fresh three-kernel comparator replay was performed here. The original
35-statement challenge does not contain the cuboid, stability, near-extremal
or forty-target extensions. A successful replay of it must not be described
as independent comparator coverage of those extensions. The repository's
existing release policy calls for replay of the final exact commit.
