Sharp four-to-three thresholds for all cuboids
============================================
Working mathematical proof, 6 November 2026

Status: working argument integrated in paper/cuboids.tex; not yet formalised in Lean.
The proof uses the established elementary corner geometry. It does not
assume the corner threshold or the previously proved rod/strip thresholds.
The accompanying programs check local geometry, lower witnesses,
and constructive controls; none of its sampled inventories is a premise
of the proof. The focused review is recorded in REVIEW.txt. Literature priority and
external mathematical refereeing remain separate tasks.

1. Statement
------------
Order the dimensions 2 <= a <= b <= c. Put

  L = 4(a+b+c)-16,
  K(c) = floor((24c-1)/5),
  T(a,b,c) = max {16a-3, L, K(c)}.

Claim (proved below):

  F_{4->3}(a,b,c) = T(a,b,c),
  G_{4->3}(a,b,c) = max {abc,16a-3,K(c)}.

The sources each use all four colours of a fixed labelled palette.
Only proper rotations are permitted. Exterior faces are monochromatic and
use exactly three colours. Internal faces need not match. The target may
depend on the inventory; selected copies must be distinct. F and G denote
least universal inventory sizes. It suffices to prove the guarantee for
exactly the stated number: from a larger inventory, first select that many
sources. Repeated varieties are allowed throughout.

2. Established geometric ingredients
------------------------------------
Use the corner-containment reduction from 68 varieties to 38. It also
preserves every edge availability, so an assembly after reduction lifts
to the original individual copies. The reduced varieties are:

* 16 ordinary varieties of each of two orientations: four T varieties,
  each in its repeated-colour bucket, and twelve P varieties, each in two
  buckets. Each ordinary source has all six bichromatic edge types and
  one cyclic orientation of every tricoloured triple. Every T_u supports
  all bicoloured corners uuv and the uu edge.
* Six checkerboards. Each lacks one distinct bichromatic pair. The
  checkerboard missing uv supports both cyclic orientations of the two
  triples other than the triples containing both u and v. Equivalently,
  it supports the triples that omit u or omit v. It has no monochromatic
  edge. In particular, it supports some triple containing a prescribed
  pair pq if and only if its missing pair is not pq.

Eight sources from a single colour bucket have a Q corner solution whose
four repeated-colour faces have that bucket colour. To recall the bucket
lemma, every reduced source in bucket u supplies at least two of the three
bicoloured corner types uuv (v!=u). For eight chosen sources, the three
supplier counts sum to at least sixteen. At least two counts are at least
four, since otherwise their sum is at most 8+3+3=14. Any two of these
supplier sets together contain all eight sources. Hall's theorem fills
four positions of each chosen type, the eight corners of Q. Choose these
eight copies before allocating any other demand.

A D target has three equal-coloured opposite face pairs. Its eight corner
positions demand four occurrences of each cyclic orientation of one
triple. If the ordinary orientation totals are A0,A1 and z_v checkerboards
support the triple omitting v, its corner Hall conditions are

  A0+z_v >= 4,  A1+z_v >= 4,  A0+A1+z_v >= 8.                 (2.1)

There are only two corner neighbourhoods: each ordinary orientation supplies
its four positions, and every eligible checkerboard can supply any of the
eight. Thus (2.1) is both necessary and sufficient, by Hall's theorem.
Call a vertex v good when (2.1) holds. In particular z_v >= 8 makes v good.
This interpretation does not assume the cuboid theorem.

Finally, an ordinary source family of a single orientation cannot supply
any target with tricoloured corners: orientation balance forces both
cyclic orientations. The only three-colour targets without tricoloured
corners are Q targets, with four faces of one colour and the two other
colours on opposite faces.

3. Three lower bounds
---------------------
Cardinality gives F >= L.

Fix one ordinary orientation and take 4a-1 copies of each of its four T
varieties. These 16a-4 sources can only assemble a Q target. The eight
corners and four monochromatic edges parallel to some axis of length d
require 8+4(d-2)=4d >= 4a sources from its repeated-colour bucket. That
bucket has only 4a-1 copies. Hence F >= 16a-3.

For the remaining bound set N0=K(c)-1 and distribute N0 sources as evenly
as possible among the six checkerboards. Each bichromatic type has at
most N0-floor(N0/6) = ceil(5N0/6) <= 4c-1 suppliers, since
5N0 <= 24c-6.
All source corners are tricoloured, so any supported three-colour target
must be D. For any axis assignment, all eight corners and the 4(c-2)
positions on the longest edges require one common bichromatic pair.
Their demand 4c exceeds its supply. Thus F >= K(c).

4. A weighted triangle lemma supplies every D extension
-------------------------------------------------------
This lemma is the main extension of the thin-box argument.

Let N >= max {29,L,K(c)}. Write

  s_a=N-4a,  s_b=N-4b,  s_c=N-4c.

Give the edges of K4 nonnegative integer weights x_e with sum <= N.
A set of vertices is designated good, is nonempty, and includes every
vertex of incident weight at least eight. Then some good vertex has a
complementary triangle whose three edge weights can be assigned to
(s_c,s_b,s_a), respectively, without exceeding those bounds.

The numerical facts used are

  s_c >= 5,  N <= 6s_c+5,
  s_b >= 7,  s_b >= 2b-1,
  s_a >= 4b-1,  s_c >= floor((4b-1)/3).                     (4.1)

Here is a direct check of (4.1).
For c<=6, N>=29 gives s_c>=5. For c>=7,
K(c)=4c+floor((4c-1)/5) >= 4c+5. Also 5K(c)>=24c-5,
which gives N<=6s_c+5.
If b<=3, N>=29 gives s_b>=17>=2b-1 and s_b>=7. If b>=4,
N>=L and a>=2,c>=b give s_b>=4b-8>=max(7,2b-1).
If c>=4 then s_a>=4(b+c)-16>=4b; if c<=3 then
s_a>=29-4a>=17>=4b-1.
If b<=3 then floor((4b-1)/3)<=3<=s_c. If b>=4 then
s_c>=4(a+b)-16>=4b-8>=floor((4b-1)/3).

If every edge has weight at most s_b, it suffices to find a good vertex
with a complementary edge of weight at most s_c. If none existed, choose
one good vertex v. Its three complementary edges would all have weight at
least s_c+1. Each of the other three vertices would consequently have
incident weight at least 2(s_c+1)>=12 and would also be good. All four
vertices would be good, forcing every edge to have weight at least s_c+1.
This contradicts sum x_e <= N <= 6s_c+5. The light edge supplies the long
axis; the other two already fit s_b and s_a.

Otherwise some edge has weight greater than s_b. Either endpoint v is
good, since that edge has weight at least s_b+1>=8. The other five edges
have total weight at most 4b-1. Sort the three edges complementary to v
as y1<=y2<=y3. Then

  y1 <= floor((4b-1)/3) <= s_c,
  y2 <= floor((4b-1)/2) = 2b-1 <= s_b,
  y3 <= 4b-1 <= s_a.

This proves the weighted triangle lemma.

Apply it to checkerboard multiplicities indexed by their missing pairs.
The incident weight z_v is exactly the checkerboard supply in (2.1).
If a D corner solution exists, the lemma gives a good triple and an axis
assignment for its D target. Any assignment of the three pairs of a colour
triple to the three edge directions is realised by a D face colouring:
permutations of the triangle's vertices induce all permutations of its
three edges. This chooses the target; each source is still placed by a
proper rotation. Each corner source possesses all three pairs
of the target triple. After reserving eight corner sources, the supply
for the pair on the d-direction edges is at least

  N-x_e-8 >= 4(d-2).

Any two distinct bichromatic supplier sets together contain every residual
source, since no reduced source misses two pairs. The total edge demand
is L-8 <= N-8. Hall's theorem therefore supplies all remaining positions.

Conclusion: at N>=max{29,L,K(c)}, the existence of ANY D corner solution
implies a complete frame, possibly after changing the D target and axes.

5. The no-D branch and two reusable constructions
-------------------------------------------------
Now take N=T(a,b,c). If no D corner solution exists, let A>=B be the ordinary
orientation totals and let C be the checkerboard total. Equation (2.1)
first gives z_v<=7, so C<=14 and A>=8. If B+z_v>=4 then (2.1) would hold.
Consequently

  B+z_v <= 3 for all v,
  4B+2C <= 12,
  M:=B+C <= 6,  A=N-M >= N-6 >= 23.                       (5.1)

Every individual checkerboard multiplicity is at most three.

Residual Hall principle: after reserving r distinct sources, each
bichromatic type has at least N-r-3 suppliers; any two different types
have all N-r residual sources as neighbours. It suffices that every
single-type demand is at most N-r-3 and the total is at most N-r.

Use colours u,v,w distinct; m=a-2,t=b-2,q=c-2. The following physical
face tuples use axis lengths (a,b,c) and order (+x,-x,+y,-y,+z,-z):

Q: (v,w,u,u,u,u).
  Monochromatic uu demand 4m, in the shortest direction.
  Bichromatic uv and uw demands each 2t+2q.
  Its eight corners and monochromatic positions can be supplied from
  4a sources of bucket u, so r=4a.

H: (v,w,u,u,u,v).
  Corner multiset 2uuv+2uuw+2uvv+uvw+uwv.
  Monochromatic uu demand 2m; vv demand t.
  Bichromatic uv demand 2m+t+2q; uw demand t+2q; vw demand t.
  Use 2a majority T_u sources, b majority T_v sources, one minority
  source supporting {u,v,w}, and one further majority ordinary source.
  These supply all corners and monochromatic positions, so r=2a+b+2.
  More explicitly, four T_u copies supply the uuv and uuw corners and
  2(a-2) further T_u copies supply uu edges. Two T_v copies supply the
  uvv corners and b-2 further T_v copies supply vv edges. Of the two
  tricoloured corners, one has the majority orientation and the other
  the opposite orientation. The extra majority and the minority source
  fill these respectively (a checkerboard supports both orientations).
  The T_u, T_v and minority supplies are disjoint; the extra majority
  copy is chosen outside the reserved copies.

Both constructions satisfy the residual single-type inequalities if

  N >= 4a+2b+2c-5.                                        (5.2)

For b+c>=6 this follows from N>=L, since
L-(4a+2b+2c-5)=2b+2c-11>=1. The remaining shapes have a=b=2,c<=3 and
N>=29 supplies (5.2) directly. Total demand is L, already bounded by N.
For H we must also check availability of the further majority source;
that check is given in the cases below.

Thus if a majority bucket has at least 4a sources, Q solves. Otherwise let
h=4a-1 and let t_u,p_u be the T count and P incidence in that bucket.
Writing n_T,n_P for majority totals gives

  t_u+p_u <= h,
  n_T+2n_P <= 4h,
  A=n_T+n_P <=4h,
  N<=16a+2,
  n_T>=2A-4h>=16a-14.                                    (5.3)

The inventory contains a minority source: otherwise N=A<=4h contradicts
N>=16a-3=4h+1. Each minority source supports some triple containing any
prescribed colour.

6. All boxes with shortest side at least three
-----------------------------------------------
Suppose a>=3. Since L<=N<=16a+2, integrality gives

  b+c <=3a+4, hence 2b<=3a+4.                              (6.1)

Some T count is at least 2a, since n_T>=16a-14>4(2a-1).
At most one colour has fewer than b T sources: two such colours would give

  n_T <=2(b-1)+2h =8a+2b-4 <=11a <16a-14,

where the strict inequality uses a>=3.
Choose a colour u with t_u>=2a. A minority source supports a triple
containing u; of its two other colours at least one, v, has t_v>=b.
Use H. The extra majority source is available because
A>=16a-9>=2a+b+1 by (6.1). Section 5 supplies the residual matching.
This proves the upper bound for every a>=3, with no aspect-ratio restriction.

7. Shortest side two
---------------------
Suppose a=2 and neither D nor Q is available. Equation (5.3) gives
N<=34, h=7 and n_T>=18. Cardinality implies b+c<=10, hence b<=5.

If b=5, then c>=5 gives N>=L>=32 and
n_T>=2(N-6)-28>=24. At most one colour has fewer than five T sources:
two such colours would give n_T<=4+4+7+7=22. A largest T count is at least
six, hence at least 2a=4. A minority triple containing that colour supplies
a second colour with at least b=5 copies. Use H.

It remains to treat b<=4, simultaneously including rods, strips and the
next family. Call a colour high if t_u>=b. There are at least two high
colours: otherwise n_T<=7+3(b-1)<=16, contradicting n_T>=18.
A largest T count is at least five.

If at least three colours are high, choose a largest-count colour u.
Any minority triple containing u has another high colour v. Thus
 t_u>=5>=2a=4 and t_v>=b, as required by H.

If exactly two colours are high, then

  n_T<=7+7+2(b-1)=2b+12<=20.

Also n_T>=2(N-M)-28>=30-2M, so M>=5. Both high colours in fact have at
least four T sources: if one had at most three, then
n_T<=7+3+2(b-1)<=16, again impossible.
If there is a minority ordinary source, it supports a triple containing
both high colours. Otherwise all M>=5 minority sources are checkerboards.
At most three miss the pair of high colours, by (5.1), so choose another
checkerboard. It supports a triple containing that pair. The two high
colours can therefore serve as u,v in H, with t_u>=4 and t_v>=b.

In every a=2 case the extra majority source is available, since
A>=23>2a+b. Section 5 supplies all residual edge positions.

This exhausts the no-D branch, and hence all cuboids. Together with the
three lower bounds, it proves the asserted frame formula.

8. Complete boxes and exact-volume assemblies
----------------------------------------------
With every side at least two, every off-frame position exposes at most
one face. Every source contains all four colours, so it can supply any
prescribed target face colour. Thus G=max{abc,F}.
Writing a=x+2,b=y+2,c=z+2 gives

  abc-L = xyz+2(xy+xz+yz) >=0,

which proves the stated simplified formula for G.

Rods 2x2xc need spare sources at every length: K(c)>4c for c>=2.
For every other ordered box ab>=6, so abc>=6c>K(c). The only remaining
volume deficit is abc<16a-3. For a>=4, a^3>=16a-3 suffices. For a=3 this
leaves (b,c)=(3,3),(3,4); for a=2,b>=3 it leaves (3,3),(3,4).
Consequently the only complete-box exceptions beyond the rods are

  2x3x3: G=29;   2x3x4: G=29;
  3x3x3: G=45;   3x3x4: G=45.

Every other cuboid can be assembled from any inventory of exactly its
volume, using every source.

A second immediate consequence identifies the exact-cardinality frames:

  F(a,b,c)=L if and only if
  b+c >= 3a+4 and c <= 5(a+b)-19.

The first inequality is L>=16a-3, with integer rounding. For the second,
L>=floor((24c-1)/5) is equivalent to 24c-1<5(L+1), hence
4c<20(a+b)-74, and therefore c<=5(a+b)-19.
Thus there is an intermediate aspect-ratio range where no frame spares
are needed; very short or very elongated shapes activate one of the other
obstructions. For the next family 2x4xc, explicitly,

  F(2,4,c) = 29 for c=4,5;
             4c+8 for 6<=c<=11;
             floor((24c-1)/5) for c>=12.

All complete 2x4xc boxes use exactly their volume, 8c.

9. Scope and next checks
------------------------
The new mathematical ingredients are the weighted three-edge selection
lemma, the shortest-axis H construction, and the high-colour argument.
Only one H orientation is needed. The proof recovers rods, strips, ordinary
cubes and the corner threshold as special cases of one argument. The proof
is an analytic extension of established corner geometry, with no new
exhaustive inventory computation.

The focused review found no mathematical gap; see REVIEW.txt for its
reasoning and limitations. Seven arithmetic interfaces have standalone
Mathlib proofs in ArithmeticReview.lean. The full geometric threshold
argument has not been formalised in Lean or externally refereed. The manuscript version is paper/cuboids.tex. The existing thin-box
formalisation retains its earlier proof route. The focused review covered
(4.1), the directional H demands, extra-majority reservation, and the
two-high-colour case; see REVIEW.txt for the dated review record.
