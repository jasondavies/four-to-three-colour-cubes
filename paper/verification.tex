\section{Verification details}\label{app:verification}
Forty-five results and the required definitions are restated in a
standalone verification file (the comparator challenge) importing only
mathlib. These include the stability and near-extremal classifications,
their counts and the forty-target extension. Failures are counted as
vectors of rotation-class multiplicities, and a vector fails when some
physical family with those multiplicities has no frame. The tables of
ordinary classes and buckets are identified with their corner-type
definitions in Lemma~\ref{lem:geometry}. Automated checks compare
the challenge statements with the proof library
and replay the proofs through the Lean, NanoDa and con-ron kernels.

The audit reports three standard axioms: \texttt{propext},
\texttt{Classical.choice} and \texttt{Quot.sound}. No proof uses holes or
\texttt{native\_decide}. The repository\footnote{\url{https://github.com/jasondavies/four-to-three-colour-cubes}.}
provides sources, reproduction commands and details of the statement checks.
The physical model and palette-independent geometry come from the shared
\texttt{colour-cubes-core} library~\cite{ColourCubes}. The challenge restates its
definitions locally.


For cuboids, \texttt{CuboidResults} proves both sharp thresholds in
Theorem~\ref{thm:cuboids} for arbitrary physical source families, together
with the no-spare criteria in Corollary~\ref{cor:cuboid-volume}.
\texttt{BoxModel} specifies exposed faces, counts positions and proves
completion. Grouped Hall matching supplies distinct copies and proper
rotations. The general upper proof follows the weighted-triangle and
$Q,H$ construction in Section~\ref{sec:cuboids}. For the lower bounds,
an equal-sided box-to-cube bridge permits reuse of the formal cube
obstruction; restricting to the longest rod permits reuse of the balanced
checkerboard obstruction. These proofs include all target orientations.

The \texttt{Cuboid} module guards nine theorem axiom reports and is imported
by the default library. The rod and strip results in \texttt{ThinResults}
retain their earlier construction, while the paper derives their formulas
as corollaries. Neither extension is in the comparator challenge.
Independent rotation-based audits check the local geometry, lower witnesses
and constructive controls. The sampled inventories supplement the written
and formal proofs; they are not an exhaustive upper-bound computation.
